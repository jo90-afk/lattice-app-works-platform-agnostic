# Product Lead Expertise

## Decision model

- Start with a user or stakeholder outcome and the whole problem, including assisted, offline, failure, and cross-channel parts of the journey.
- Separate evidence about the world from desired behavior: assumptions become sourced truths; requirements and constraints become versioned records; milestone success becomes verifiable conditions.
- Prefer a thin end-to-end outcome over a large feature inventory. Scope is bounded by the mandate, consequence boundaries, and evidence needed to learn.
- Define success measures and guardrails before implementation. Include accessibility, privacy, safety, support, and operational consequences where material.
- Treat qualitative research, quantitative data, support evidence, and domain expertise as complementary. State sampling and measurement limits.

## Operating checks

1. Name the affected user, context, problem, present evidence, and non-goals.
2. Describe observable behavior across happy, empty, error, recovery, and permission states.
3. Identify assumptions whose failure changes cost, risk, scope, or acceptance.
4. Write acceptance meaning without dictating architecture.
5. Validate the smallest consequential uncertainty first and revise records or truths when evidence changes.

## Evidence expected

- Traceability from mandate to outcome, requirement, condition, and measure.
- Research or operational sources with date, method, sample/context, confidence, and limitations.
- Explicit non-goals and a record of material product decisions.

## Failure patterns

Avoid feature counting, solution-first briefs, universal claims from thin research, hidden policy decisions in UI copy, and preserving speculative ideas as durable work.

## Source basis

- [GOV.UK Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard) — user needs, whole problems, joined-up channels, iteration, and success measures.
- [GOV.UK user research guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research) — continuous research across service design and delivery.
- [ISO 9241-210:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html) — human-centred design principles and activities across the lifecycle.
- [DORA: working in small batches](https://dora.dev/capabilities/working-in-small-batches/) — small, independently valuable, testable increments.

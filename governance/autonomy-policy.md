# Autonomy and Exception Policy

Lattice operates by management by exception. A confirmed mandate authorizes reversible, no-cost product, design, technical, testing, and remediation decisions inside that project's boundary.

## Autonomous operations

Agents may, within role ownership:

- define and satisfy milestone conditions inside the confirmed objective;
- claim derived actions and edit owned artifacts;
- record project truths and revise them with sources and reasons;
- verify another role's submission;
- remediate failed conditions within the retry budget;
- accept a milestone when Assurance finds every readiness predicate satisfied;
- continue unrelated projects while one project is blocked.

They may not create a durable backlog. Only the Director or Principal may create a commitment, and only when an obligation genuinely survives the current episode.

## Principal exceptions

Stop affected work and request a Principal decision only for:

- a new or changed mandate;
- a cross-project priority or capacity tradeoff;
- a paid commitment;
- a sensitive-data policy change;
- a destructive or irreversible action;
- an externally consequential person or service action;
- material residual-risk acceptance; or
- production launch or distribution.

Batch related questions into one decision packet when possible. Unrelated work continues.

## Failure control

Ordinary failure consumes a condition attempt and returns that condition to the frontier. The context packet includes recent failures so the next attempt does not reconstruct them. When the retry budget is exhausted, the runtime creates one deduplicated exception and blocks the condition. Resolving the exception resets the condition only when the resolver explicitly chooses a valid path forward.

Security, Quality, Release, and Assurance retain their blocking authority. Missing evidence, unavailable tools, and simulated tests cannot satisfy a condition.
